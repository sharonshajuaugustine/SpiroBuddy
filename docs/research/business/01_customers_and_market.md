# Customers and market size in India

## Answer in 10 lines

1. **The buyer is the hospital, not the patient.** The person who *wants* it is the heart-surgery or physiotherapy head; the person who *signs* is the purchase head for small items and the CEO/owner for big ones (L.E.K. survey of 100 Indian hospital executives, 2025, S14).
2. **Start small enough to skip the committee.** In central-government hospitals, anything up to ₹50,000 can be bought without quotations and ₹50,000–₹5 lakh needs only a three-person local purchase committee (finance ministry order, 10 Jul 2024, S4). A 20-unit pilot fits under the first line. Private hospitals set their own limits; none are public.
3. **Best first paying customer:** a mid-size private or trust/mission hospital (roughly 100–300 beds) that does heart-bypass and big abdominal operations. One owner decides, no tender, and extra bed-days hurt them directly.
4. **Best first pilot site (for evidence, not money):** the heart-surgery unit of a teaching hospital. Example: Government Medical College Kottayam does 1,000–1,360 open-heart operations a year at over 100% bed occupancy, with one physiotherapist listed for the department (S12).
5. **Best region to start: Kerala**, unless the team lives elsewhere. Kerala lets government departments buy from registered startups **up to ₹50 lakh with no tender** (S19), has the most nurses per person in India (ledger #67) and about 3,300 hospitals (S1). Karnataka is the bigger market but the most crowded; Tamil Nadu is strong but buys centrally.
6. **Market size, honestly:** India-wide about **₹25 crore (cautious) / ₹74 crore (middle) / ₹147 crore (optimistic) a year** if every suitable surgery patient were enrolled. What a student team could win in three years is **₹3 lakh / ₹38 lakh / ₹2 crore a year**. It is a real but small market on post-surgery alone.
7. **The biggest unknown** is what share of operations get an incentive spirometer at all. No Indian figure exists. We assumed 10% / 20% / 30%.
8. **Nobody could tell us how many incentive spirometers India buys a year.** The only counted figure found is one Indian Army order for 1,611 units (June 2026, S16).
9. **What buyers are used to paying is low.** Dozee, the best-known Indian patient-monitoring company, raised about US$20 million yet earned only ₹5.2 crore from operations in 2023–24, with a ₹68 crore loss (S9). Selling monitoring to Indian hospitals is slow and thin.
10. **Insurers and home-care firms are second-wave customers.** Star Health already pays Portea, Care24 and Apollo for home care, but for fevers and infections, not post-surgery rehab (S13). Portea earns ₹160 crore a year, 59% from services (S5).

## Findings

### 1. Customer map

### B1-1: Four different people are involved, and they want different things
- Detail (our mapping, built from the sources below):

| Role | Who | What they care about |
|---|---|---|
| **User** | Patient (often 55–65, ledger #114); ward nurse; physiotherapist; a family member at home | Easy, spoken in their language, no extra work for the nurse |
| **Chooser** (asks for it) | Cardiothoracic surgeon, anaesthetist, head of physiotherapy, nursing superintendent | Fewer lung problems, proof patients did the exercise, no new workload |
| **Buyer** (signs) | Purchase/materials head for small items; CEO or owner for large ones; biomedical engineering checks safety | Price, doctor's preference, "is it Indian-made", after-sales support |
| **Payer** (money comes from) | The hospital, out of a fixed package (PM-JAY ₹1,62,400 for a bypass, ledger #128) or out of the patient's bill; rarely an insurer | Shorter stay, fewer ICU days (ICU day ≈ ₹25,927, ledger #142) |

- Source: L.E.K. Consulting, India Hospital Insights Survey, 19 Sep 2025, 100 hospital executives – S14 – grade B. Ledger #128, #141, #142.
- Status: SINGLE SOURCE for who signs; the table itself is team reasoning.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: pitch the surgeon or physio head first (they create the demand), but bring a one-page price sheet for the purchase head.

### B1-2: In Indian hospitals the CEO decides big purchases, the purchase head decides cheap ones, and doctors sway the middle
- Detail: Survey of 100 Indian hospital executives (2025). The CEO is the most influential buyer, most of all for costly equipment. Procurement directors are the key decision-makers for low-cost products. Practising doctors influence mid-priced purchases, especially in investor-owned hospitals. Top buying criteria: cost (42–62%), doctor preference (39–56%), innovativeness (32–48%). About 45% of hospitals report limits on imported products or a push to buy Indian-made (60% in Delhi). The survey covers private hospitals; it says government money is under 5% of their revenue.
- Source: S14 – grade B.
- Status: SINGLE SOURCE.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: a low-cost Indian-made unit sits in the "purchase head decides" band. "Made in India" is a selling point. Do not expect a CEO meeting for a ₹50,000 pilot.

### B1-3: Government spend limits (central rules, July 2024): up to ₹50,000 no quotation; up to ₹5 lakh a local committee; above ₹10 lakh a bid on GeM
- Detail: GeM is the Government e-Marketplace, the online shop government offices must buy from. Finance ministry order F.1/3/2024-PPD dated 10 Jul 2024 doubled the limits in the General Financial Rules:

| Rule | What it allows | Limit now (was) |
|---|---|---|
| 154 | Buy without any quotation | up to ₹50,000 (₹25,000) |
| 155 | Buy on a 3-member Local Purchase Committee's certificate | ₹50,000–₹5 lakh (₹25,000–₹2.5 lakh) |
| 149(i) | GeM direct purchase from any seller | up to ₹50,000 |
| 149(ii) | GeM: lowest price among at least 3 makers | ₹50,000–₹10 lakh |
| 149(iii) | GeM: bidding or reverse auction compulsory | above ₹10 lakh (₹5 lakh) |
| 162 | Limited tender (invited sellers only) | up to ₹50 lakh (₹25 lakh) |
| 161 | Open advertised tender | ₹50 lakh and above |

- Source: reproduction of the Department of Expenditure order – S4 – grade B (third-party copy of a government order; we did not open the order on the ministry's own site).
- Status: SINGLE SOURCE (search results showed the same figures on IIT Kanpur and Delhi University copies, snippet only).
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: these rules bind central-government hospitals (AIIMS and similar). **State hospitals and medical colleges follow their own state store-purchase rules, which we did not check.** Our reading is that a 20-unit pilot at the team's own parts estimate of ₹1,000–1,700 a unit stays under ₹50,000; confirm with the hospital's stores officer before quoting. Not legal advice.

### B1-6: Hospitals say they plan to spend on home-based care and digital tools, but budget is a named barrier
- Detail: Of 100 Indian hospital executives (2025): 49% plan to invest in home-based long-term care; 52% plan to expand bariatric (weight-loss) surgery; 37% name staff shortage as a top priority and 34% new IT systems. They expect digital tools to cut medical errors (43%) and raise staff efficiency (41%). Barriers: fear of replacing jobs (33%), weak IT (30%), budget (29%).
- Source: S14 – grade B.
- Status: SINGLE SOURCE.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: sell it as "saves nurse time during a staff shortage" and "extends care to the home". Avoid any wording that sounds like replacing the physiotherapist.

### B1-7: Insurers already pay for care at home, through the same home-care firms we might partner with
- Detail: Star Health's Home Health Care started July 2023 and reached 100 locations by Feb 2025. Over 15,000 patients; cashless; a partner doctor visits within three hours; under 1% needed hospital admission. Partners: Care24, Portea, Argala, Athulya, Apollo. Conditions: viral fever, dengue, typhoid, stomach infection, respiratory infection. The insurance regulator has allowed "home treatment" as an add-on cover since 2021 (snippet only, C).
- Source: Angel One news report of the company announcement, 25 Feb 2025 – S13 – grade B/C.
- Status: SINGLE SOURCE.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: an insurer route exists, but it is for acute illness and runs through home-care companies. It is a year-two conversation, not a first customer.

### 3. Market size, built from counted things

**Plain words first.**
- **TAM** (total addressable market) = the money if *every* suitable patient in India used it.
- **SAM** (serviceable market) = the part we could realistically reach with our model: private hospitals in the five southern states.
- **SOM** (obtainable market) = what a small team could actually win in about three years.

### B1-13: Recommendation: Kerala, heart-surgery units, starting with one teaching hospital and one mission/private hospital
- Detail: Why Kerala: (1) a legal shortcut for startups to sell to government without a tender, up to ₹50 lakh; (2) the best nurse numbers in India, so there is someone to hand out the device; (3) dense mid-size private and mission hospitals (about 2,000 private, estimated); (4) a named, busy public heart unit to pilot in; (5) an older population (not sourced this round). Why heart surgery: stay is long (10 days built into the PM-JAY package, ledger #128), the exercise is routine, and the reminder trial that anchors our evidence was in bypass patients (ledger #2).
- The ₹50 lakh route needs the startup to be **registered with Kerala Startup Mission, and "Demand Day" is open only to startups registered in Kerala** (S19). Reports say the 2023 update also asks for central DPIIT startup recognition (snippet only, C).
- **If the team is based in Karnataka or Tamil Nadu, start at home instead.** Being able to visit the ward weekly matters more than any table above.
- Source: S19 – grade B (official state page); S12, S1.
- Status: SINGLE SOURCE for the rule; we could not confirm it is unchanged as of Oct 2026. Our reading of the rule is as above; confirm with Kerala Startup Mission before relying on it.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: registering the company in Kerala could be worth more than any grant, because it opens a no-tender sales route.

### B1-15: What a bed-day and an operation are worth (already in the ledger)
- Detail: Ward bed-day ≈ ₹2,307 and ICU bed-day ≈ ₹25,927 (cost to the hospital; ledger #141, #142). PM-JAY pays ₹1,62,400 for a bypass and ₹32,900 for lung-lobe removal (ledger #128). A 2016 review independently put the public-scheme payment for lung-lobe removal at about US$750 (S6).
- Status: CONFIRMED (ledger) for the amounts; the link to SpiroBuddy is a team argument.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: at ₹750 a patient, the hospital breaks even if one patient in about 35 avoids a single ICU day (₹25,927 ÷ ₹750, team arithmetic). **We have no evidence that it does avoid one.** Say "this is what a pilot would test".

### B1-16: No public per-bed or per-patient price exists for remote monitoring in India
- Detail: Dozee, Cloudphysician, Tricog and Qure.ai publish no prices. Dozee's 2020 interview describes a free demo and trial, then installation (S3). Search results describe it as a subscription with no upfront equipment cost (snippet only).
- Status: NOT CONFIRMED.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: hospitals expect a **free trial first** and a **pay-as-you-use** model. Plan for both.

## What to say in the 5-minute pitch (5 bullets max, each with its source)

1. "Our customer is the hospital. The heart-surgery or physiotherapy head asks for it; for a low-cost Indian-made device the purchase head signs." (S14)
2. "India's public records show about 49 lakh major operations a year. If even one in five of those patients used SpiroBuddy at ₹750 each, that is about ₹74 crore a year. Both of those inputs are our assumptions, and a pilot is how we test them." (ledger #63; B1-10)
3. "We'd start with heart-surgery units in Kerala: one government teaching unit there does over a thousand open-heart operations a year at more than 100% occupancy, with one physiotherapist listed." (S12)

5. "The whole recovery would cost a hospital about what one home physiotherapy visit costs a family: around ₹1,000." (S18; price is a team assumption)

## What NOT to say (claims that would be wrong or unproven)

- **Any "USD billion" market figure.** We opened no such report.
- **"India does X lakh bypass operations a year"** as a fact. The only figures are from 2007 (60,000 open-heart) and a 2017 industry speaker (1.5–2 lakh). There is no national registry.
- **"X lakh incentive spirometers are sold in India a year."** No figure exists.
- **"Hospitals will pay ₹500–1,000 per patient."** No hospital has been asked.
- **"Insurers will pay for this."** Star Health's home care covers infections, not post-surgery rehab.
- **"Dozee proves the model works."** It proves hospitals will *install* monitoring; its accounts show a ₹68 crore loss on ₹5.2 crore revenue.
- **"69,265 hospitals" or "43,487 private hospitals" as official counts.** The private number is a 2020 model estimate.
- **"Government hospitals can buy from us without a tender"** as a general statement. It is one state's rule (Kerala), for startups registered there, and we have not confirmed it is unchanged.
- **Any sales-cycle length as a fact.** Ours are guesses.
