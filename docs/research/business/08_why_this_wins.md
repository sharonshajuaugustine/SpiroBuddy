## The thesis in 5 sentences

1. After a big operation, Indian hospitals hand patients a cheap plastic breathing trainer (an incentive spirometer) and then have no way of knowing whether it is used. US nurses say most patients don't use it properly (86%), and no major trial ever recorded whether patients actually did the exercise.
2. SpiroBuddy keeps that same ₹100–1,000 device and clips a small laser base under it. The base counts every breath without touching the air the patient breathes. It coaches the patient by voice in their own language, shows the nurse who is falling behind, and sends opted-in family members a WhatsApp update.
3. The timing is right. Ayushman Bharat pays a fixed price per operation that already includes 15 days of follow-up after discharge, and since 2024 it covers every Indian aged 70 and over. Nurses are scarce, and voice AI in Indian languages now costs paise per message.
4. The pattern is proven elsewhere. Measured reminders doubled breathing-exercise use in a US trial, electronic trackers lifted inhaler use by 19 percentage points across six trials, and Indian hospitals already pay for no-touch monitoring that saves nurse time.
5. We start narrow: heart-surgery units, a lent unit and about ₹1,000 per patient episode (less than half of one ward bed-day). We then grow into lung rehab for the lakhs of TB survivors each year. The honest risk is that we still have to show it changes recovery, and our pilot is built to measure exactly that.

## 1. Why now: tailwinds in India, 2024–2026

### W-1: The government insurer now covers every Indian aged 70+, and pays by fixed package
- **Detail:** In September 2024 the Cabinet extended Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY to all citizens aged 70 and over, with **no income limit**, aiming at about **6 crore senior citizens in 4.5 crore families**. Over 10 lakh enrolled in the first three weeks. By 17 Nov 2024 the scheme had **30,745 empanelled hospitals** and **6.5 crore authorised hospital admissions** (₹81,979 crore). Each package covers pre-hospitalisation costs and **"post-hospitalisation follow-up care up to 15 days"**. Later enrolment counts (about 1 crore+ cards) appear only on secondary sites that disagree with each other.
- **Source:** PIB, "Ayushman Bharat: Vay Vandana Cards achieves Milestone", 17 Nov 2024 (W3) – **A**. Later counts: search snippets – **C**.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED (primary government document) for the scheme rules and 2024 numbers; NOT CONFIRMED for 2026 enrolment totals.
- **Why it matters:** the patients most likely to need help with breathing exercise (older, often with low literacy) are now insured. Their hospital is paid **one fixed price** that already includes 15 days of follow-up. A complication or a slow recovery costs the hospital, not the scheme.

### W-2: India's digital health ID system has gone mass-market
- **Detail:** In May 2026, **over 100 crore health records were linked to more than 90 crore ABHA accounts** (ABHA = the 14-digit national health ID). Over 23.21 crore ABHA-linked hospital queue tokens had been issued by 18 June 2026. The government's Digital Health Incentive Scheme had paid **hospitals ₹107+ crore** and digital-solution companies ₹26+ crore for creating linked digital records. A Lok Sabha reply (snippet only) put ABHA IDs at 95.99 crore by 31 July 2026.
- **Source:** PIB backgrounder, 6 July 2026 (W2) – **A**; Lok Sabha figure – snippet, **C**.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED (PIB) for the May 2026 milestone.
- **Why it matters:** a hospital's digital records are now paid for by the government, so a clean post-surgery adherence record has somewhere to go later. **Honest limit:** ABHA accounts created is not the same as people actively using them, and ABDM is **not needed** for a pilot (BUSINESS_BRIEF, ICMR section).

### W-3: Too few nurses to check every patient every hour
- **Detail:** India has **2.6 registered nursing staff per 1,000 people**, below the WHO benchmark of 3 (Rajya Sabha reply, July 2026). Ward-level nurse-to-patient ratios are not measured nationally: a review says Indian staffing norms are "not updated" and lack workload research; the "1:20" figure seen online comes from MPs' speeches, not a survey.
- **Source:** #67 (F-INDIA-7) – **B**; nurse-norm review and parliamentary debates – search snippets, **C**.
- **Status:** SINGLE SOURCE (2.6 per 1,000); ward ratios NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Why it matters:** an hourly exercise can't be supervised by hand. Hospitals told L.E.K. that staff shortage is a top priority (37%) and that they expect digital tools to raise staff efficiency (41%) (B1-6, **B**).

### W-4: Phones are in the house, and the house shares them
- **Detail:** India had **958 million active internet users** in 2025 (up about 8%); **548 million are rural**, and rural use is growing about four times faster than urban. **18% go online through someone else's phone** (nearly 80% of those are rural), and 579 million people (38%) are still not active users. 44% of users already use AI features such as voice search.
- **Source:** IAMAI–Kantar *Internet in India 2025*, reported by Business Today, 29 Jan 2026 (W5) – **B**.
- **Status:** SINGLE SOURCE (one survey, 87,000+ people; widely reported).
- **Why it matters:** the family phone is the real channel. This backs our design: the patient never has to own a smartphone (only 41% of older Indians do, F-INDIA-5), and the family member's WhatsApp gets the update. No official WhatsApp user count for India exists; the much-quoted "535 million" is a third-party estimate (**C**, NOT CONFIRMED), so don't quote it.

### W-5: Indian-language voice AI became cheap and state-backed
- **Detail:** Sarvam AI was picked in the IndiaAI Mission's first phase to build an Indian foundation model: **one of 43 proposals chosen from 500+**, with the mission's largest compute subsidy, **₹98.68 crore for 4,096 Nvidia H100 GPUs**, and a promise to open-source the models. The IndiaAI Mission budget is about US$1.2 billion (launched March 2024). Sarvam's text-to-speech costs ₹3 per 1,000 characters across 10 Indian languages plus English, with a free MIT-licensed offline backup (AI4Bharat).
- **Source:** Outlook Business, 16 Jul 2025 (W13) – **B**; Sarvam pricing (#53) – **B**; F-VOICE-10.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED (selection reported widely; price from the vendor's page). Funding-round size: NOT CONFIRMED (reports disagree).
- **Why it matters:** a voice coach in Tamil or Bengali now costs a few paise per message, and there is a free fallback if the service goes away.

### W-6: India wants Indian-made medical devices
- **Detail:** India's medical-device market is put at **US$15.2 billion (2025), forecast US$50.1 billion by 2030**. Exports were US$4.01 billion in FY25. The government has a PLI (production-linked incentive) scheme for devices and four medical-device parks, **one of them in Kerala**. The National Medical Devices Policy 2023 aims to cut import dependence, which press and industry reports put at **70–80%** (snippets only). A July 2026 Lok Sabha reply (snippet) says the device PLI has a ₹3,420 crore outlay and 27 approved projects.
- **Source:** IBEF infographic, Feb 2026 (W12) – **B** (forecast); import share and PLI detail – snippets, **C**.
- **Status:** SINGLE SOURCE (market figure, a forecast); import share NOT CONFIRMED.
- **Why it matters:** 45% of Indian hospitals report a push to buy Indian-made (L.E.K., B1-2, **B**). **Honest limit:** PLI pays big manufacturers in four segments; it does not fund us. Our real government money is NIDHI-PRAYAS (up to ₹10 lakh) and BIRAC BIG (up to ₹50 lakh) (B3, **A**).

### W-7: TB elimination and an ageing country create a second wave of lung patients
- **Detail:** India notifies about 26 lakh TB patients a year and 90% finish treatment (#161, **A**). NTEP asks for every one to be checked for lung damage, and the 2024 RBIPMT rehab guidance lists the incentive spirometer among breathing exercises (F-VIS-2, F-VIS-7, **A**). Separately, India's over-60 population is growing **about 41% per decade**, will be **over 20% of the population by 2050**, and will outnumber children by about 2046; the over-80s grow about 279% between 2022 and 2050.
- **Source:** UNFPA India, *India Ageing Report 2023* news release (W14) – **B**; PIB and NTEP as above – **A**.
- **Status:** CONFIRMED.
- **Why it matters:** more older patients, more surgery on older bodies, and a government programme that already asks for lung rehab after TB.

### W-8: Recovery is moving out of the hospital
- **Detail:** India's recorded major surgeries are about 4.9 million a year and grow about 4% a year, with large unmet need (#63, F-INDIA-1, **A**; an undercount, as private hospitals are missed). Enhanced-recovery (ERAS) programmes shorten stays: a meta-analysis of 13 cardiac-surgery trials (1,704 patients) found shorter ICU and hospital stays (search snippet, **C**). Under a fixed package, a shorter stay is the hospital's profit, which pushes recovery home sooner.
- **Status:** surgery count CONFIRMED (#63); ERAS effect NOT CONFIRMED this round (snippet).
- **Why it matters:** the exercise used to happen on a ward with a nurse nearby. Increasingly it happens at home with nobody watching. That is the gap SpiroBuddy fills.

| Domain | Finding | Source, grade, status |
|---|---|---|
| Breathing exercise after bypass (USA, randomised trial, 145 patients) | Hours with exercise done: 58% vs 28%; breaths a day 35 vs 17; slightly less lung collapse on X-ray | *JAMA Surg* 2019 (F-ADH-1) – **A**, SINGLE SOURCE |

| Inhalers, children (randomised trial, 220, New Zealand) | Adherence median **84% vs 30%** with an audiovisual reminder device; asthma symptom score improved more (p=0.008); school absence unchanged | Chan, *Lancet Respir Med* 2015 (W1) – **A**, SINGLE SOURCE |
| Home physio after hip replacement (randomised trial, 240, Shanghai) | Wearable sensors with real-time feedback: **more sessions completed (p=.002)** and more follow-up participation (p<.001); function gain significant but below the "clinically important" threshold; similar costs | Jiang et al., *JMIR mHealth* 2026 (W9) – **A**, SINGLE SOURCE |
| TB medicines, 76 studies | Digital adherence tools: modest effect (OR 1.14, low certainty); side effects reported to staff sooner (OR 1.57) | F-ADH-8 – **A** |
| TB, India's 99DOTS (597 patients, Mumbai, Chennai, Vellore) | Self-reported "missed call" dosing matched urine tests only **69%** of the time; NPV 21%: of patients flagged as missing doses, only about 2 in 10 truly were | Thomas/Subbaraman, *Clin Infect Dis* 2020 (W4) – **A**, SINGLE SOURCE |

**The lesson, honestly stated:**
1. Measurement plus reminders reliably **raises how often people do the thing**. This holds across breathing exercise, inhalers and physiotherapy.
2. It does **not automatically improve clinical outcomes**. The Cochrane inhaler review says so plainly. That is why we never claim outcome benefit and why the pilot must measure it.
3. 99DOTS shows that **self-reported adherence is unreliable in India**. People forget to report, and some report doses they didn't take. SpiroBuddy's sensor records each breath, so the nurse sees what happened, not what was claimed. This is our clearest technical answer to "India tried digital adherence for TB and it was patchy."

## 6. Business-model strength: who pays and why

1. **Under a fixed package, a complication is the hospital's loss.** PM-JAY pays ₹1,62,400 for a bypass with 10 days built in (#128, **A**). The package also covers follow-up care up to 15 days after discharge (W3, **A**). Every extra ward day (₹2,307 average) or ICU day (₹25,927 at PGIMER) comes out of that fixed sum (F-BIZ-4/5, **A**). The PM-JAY payer base just grew to include every Indian aged 70+ (W1).
2. **Our price sits below one ward day.** About ₹1,000 per 14-day episode, unit lent and reused (plan 03, team estimate). Our cost is about ₹670 an episode in a pilot and about ₹470 at scale (plan 03 §2, team estimate), so roughly ₹330–530 is left per episode before salaries.
3. **Break-even for the hospital (team arithmetic, not evidence):** at ₹1,000 a patient, the hospital breaks even if **one patient in about 26 avoids one ICU day** (₹25,927 ÷ ₹1,000), or if the average stay falls by under half a ward day. **We have no evidence yet that it does either.** The pilot measures it.
4. **The comparison families understand:** the whole recovery costs about what **one** home physiotherapy visit costs (₹1,000, Tata 1mg, B1-14, **B**).
5. **Hospitals already buy this kind of thing:** Manipal pays for home monitoring after bypass (#130); Dozee is in 200+ hospitals selling "nursing hours saved" (W11). Purchases under ₹50,000 need no quotation in central-government hospitals (B1-3, **B**), and a 20-unit pilot can sit under that line.
6. **Later payers, honestly ranked:** home-care chains and rehab chains (Portea, Sukino); drug companies' patient-support budgets (Cipla, Lupin) as partners; TB and CSR money for government hospitals; insurers last (Star Health's home care covers infections, not surgery rehab, B1-7).
7. **Indian complication-cost data:** still no direct rupee cost of a post-surgery lung complication in India. The proxies are ventilator pneumonia at PGIMER (₹5.44 lakh vs ₹2.07 lakh without it) and the Udaipur stay data (F-BIZ-5, F-CLIN-8). Present them as anchors, never as savings.

## Lines to never say (additions to the existing lists)

These add to `docs/research/PITCH_FACTS.md` "Never say" and `docs/plan/06_LEGAL_DEEP_DIVE.md` "Do not say". Everything already banned there stays banned (for example "prevents pneumonia", "reduces complications", "₹X saved per patient", "first", "partner hospitals", "real-time monitoring", "detects deterioration").

1. **"Trackers and reminders improve health outcomes."** The Cochrane inhaler review found adherence rose but outcomes didn't clearly improve. Say "they raise how often people do it."
2. **"Ayushman Bharat will pay for SpiroBuddy"** or "PM-JAY reimburses us." No package line exists for it. Say "the hospital is paid a fixed price, so it gains if recovery goes well."
3. **"The 15-day follow-up in PM-JAY funds our service."** It covers post-discharge care costs inside the package; it is not money earmarked for us.
4. **"Every senior is now covered"** without "aged 70+, under PM-JAY". And no 2026 enrolment count: the figures conflict.
5. **"96 crore Indians use ABDM."** These are accounts created, not active users. And never "ABDM-integrated" (we aren't).
6. **"958 million Indians are online, so patients can use our app."** 38% are still offline, and 18% of users borrow someone's phone. Our design point is that the patient needs no smartphone.
7. **"India has 535 million WhatsApp users"** or any India WhatsApp count. Meta publishes none; third-party figures disagree.
8. **"Dozee saved 14 million nursing hours"** as a fact. Say "Dozee says…" (it is the company's own claim).
9. **"Government-backed voice AI"** or "Sarvam is our partner." Say "a company selected under the IndiaAI Mission". We are only a paying customer of its public API.
10. **"PLI / medical-device parks support us."** They fund large manufacturers in four segments. Our grants are NIDHI-PRAYAS and BIRAC.
11. **"The global remote-monitoring market is US$X billion"** or any incentive-spirometer market size. Paid reports, seen only as snippets, disagree by several times.
12. **"Digital adherence is proven in India (99DOTS)."** Its accuracy was 69%. Use 99DOTS only as the reason we measure instead of asking.
13. **"Sensor feedback is proven for rehab"** as a general claim. One Chinese hip trial showed more sessions; the function gain was below the clinically important level.
14. **"India's elderly will double, so our market doubles."** The population figure is real; the market link is not.
15. **"Nurse-to-patient ratio is 1:20 in Indian wards."** It comes from MPs' speeches, not a survey. Use 2.6 nurses per 1,000 people (Rajya Sabha).
16. **"Hospitals break even if one in 26 patients avoids an ICU day"** without "our arithmetic, untested". It is a threshold, not a result.
