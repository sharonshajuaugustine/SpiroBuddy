# Sales and go-to-market: how SpiroBuddy starts selling in India
**Written:** 10 Oct 2026 · **Sources opened:** 18 (plus 5 that failed to load, and search snippets, all labelled) · **Confidence overall:** medium for government rules, **low** for private-hospital buying habits, prices and dealer margins (little is published)

Words used here, once:
- **GeM** = Government e-Marketplace, the central government's online shop for all public buyers.
- **EMD** = Earnest Money Deposit, the refundable deposit a bidder pays to enter a tender.
- **DPIIT** = the government department that officially "recognises" startups.
- **MSE** = micro or small enterprise (registered on the Udyam portal).
- **OEM** = the original maker of a product (as opposed to a reseller).
- **CDSCO** = India's medical-device regulator.
- **CSR** = Corporate Social Responsibility, the share of profit large Indian companies must spend on social causes.
- **PM-JAY** = Ayushman Bharat, the government health insurance scheme.

---

## Answer in 10 lines

1. **We cannot sell yet.** The report expects the clip-on to be a Class B medical device (section 7.1). Until it has a CDSCO manufacturing licence, every deployment must be a free research pilot, not a sale. Our reading; confirm with a regulatory consultant.
2. **Start with private and trust teaching hospitals, not government tenders.** Over 90% of government medtech buying goes through price tenders, which do not suit a one-of-a-kind product (B2-1). Government suppliers in several states wait many months to two years for payment (B2-4).
3. **GeM comes later, and needs the licence.** GeM's own policy treats a "valid manufacturing licence" as the proof for medical-device makers (policy v3.2, Jan 2024) (B2-5).
4. **Startup relaxations are real but not guaranteed.** Central rules let buyers waive EMD, past turnover and past experience for DPIIT-recognised startups (GFR 2017 rules 170(i) and 173(i)). The Delhi High Court ruled in July 2022 that a hospital may refuse them (B2-6, B2-7).
5. **The model that fits a reused ₹1,000–1,700 unit: the hospital pays a fee per patient episode, and we keep owning the device.** It mirrors what Tricog and Qure.ai did (pay per use) and avoids a capital-purchase approval (B2-13, B2-14, B2-19).
6. **For government hospitals, use CSR or grant money, not the hospital's budget.** Dozee did exactly this with "MillionICU" (B2-12). Indian companies spent ₹34,908.75 crore on CSR in 2023–24 (B2-18).
7. **Indian medtech startups took 1–2 years or more to reach a first commercial deployment**, even with strong teams (Qure.ai: about 14 months; Niramai: about 2 years to a first product; Dozee: about 4 years) (B2-11 to B2-16).
8. **Nobody publishes hospital prices or pilot terms.** We found no public per-bed or per-patient price for Dozee, Cloudphysician or Stasis. Do not quote one.
9. **Channels to test:** Romsons (the spirometer maker, 1,500+ distributors) as a bundling partner, and one cardiothoracic conference: IACTSCON, 18–21 Feb 2027, Kolkata (B2-21, B2-22).
10. **First goal: 1 signed pilot letter and a named clinical champion within 90 days, 10 pilot sites in 12–18 months.** These are team targets, not sourced benchmarks (B2-24, B2-25).

## Findings

### 1. How Indian hospitals buy

### B2-1: Government hospitals buy almost everything by tender, on lowest price
- Detail: "Over 90%" of government medtech procurement goes through tenders where several firms bid on a standard product, mostly on price. The author (head of an Indian medtech company) says this "does not work for an innovative product" made by one company, and causes delays or rejection. He also says medtech takes 3–5 years to develop and another 3–4 years to reach commercial success. Opinion piece, May 2019.
- Source: "How startups are solving challenges plaguing Indian healthcare", Siraj Dhanani (InnAccel), BioSpectrum India, 27 May 2019 – https://biospectrumindia.com/views/17/13719/how-startups-are-solving-challenges-plaguing-indian-healthcare.html – grade B (industry opinion, no data shown)
- Status: SINGLE SOURCE
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: a tender needs several competing suppliers of the same thing. We are alone in our category, so tenders are the wrong first door.

### B2-2: In central government hospitals, equipment above ₹1 crore is bought centrally; below that the hospital buys it itself
- Detail: A Health Ministry "Procurement Cell" note says equipment worth more than ₹1 crore goes through the Ministry's cell and an Integrated Purchase Committee; purchases up to ₹1 crore are delegated to the hospitals. The hospital starts the process with a request ("indent"), an approval of the money, and technical specifications. Date of the note not seen.
- Source: MoHFW "Procurement Cell" PDF (main.mohfw.gov.in) – **search snippet only, the site did not load** – grade A source, C for how we read it
- Status: NOT CONFIRMED (snippet only)
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: our device is tiny in value, so the decision sits inside one hospital. That is good: one department head can champion it.

### B2-3: Private-hospital buying steps, sales-cycle length and credit terms are NOT published in any reliable source we found

### B2-4: Government hospitals pay suppliers very late in several states

- Source: "Telangana: Hospital Suppliers Halt Aarogyasri Supplies Over Dues", Deccan Chronicle, 2 Sep 2026 – https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/telangana-hospital-suppliers-halt-aarogyasri-supplies-over-dues-1984361 – grade B. Kerala (ocacademy.in) and Jammu (greaterkashmir.com) – snippets only, grade C.
- Status: CONFIRMED for the pattern (three states, one opened); amounts for Kerala and Jammu are snippet-only
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: a tiny team cannot wait two years for money. Government sites should be paid for by a CSR donor or a grant, up front.

### B2-4a: The law says a registered micro or small supplier must be paid within 45 days
- Detail: Under the MSMED Act 2006 (sections 15–16), a buyer must pay a registered micro or small enterprise by the agreed date, which cannot be later than 45 days from acceptance. Late payment carries compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate. Since the Finance Act 2023 (section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act, applying from 2024), a buyer that pays late also loses its tax deduction until it pays. This applies to micro and small suppliers with Udyam registration.
- Source: legal summaries (livelaw.in, scconline.com, taxmann.com, tallysolutions.com) – **search snippets only; the Act itself was not opened** – grade B/C
- Status: CONFIRMED by several secondary sources; statute text not read
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: register as a micro enterprise (Udyam) once the company exists. It gives a legal 45-day limit to quote in contracts. Confirm with a chartered accountant.

### 2. Government buying

### B2-5: GeM treats a valid manufacturing licence as the credential for a medical-device maker
- Detail: GeM's "Vendor Validation Policy" version 3.2 (file dated 10 Jan 2024) says seller identity, turnover, GST, company registration, Udyam and DPIIT startup status are checked online. Makers and resellers are then checked by an outside agency (RITES Ltd) every 3 years. The list of sellers **exempt** from that outside check includes "Medical Device manufacturer with 'Valid Manufacturing License' from the issuing Licensing Authority". The assessment fee for a maker with turnover up to ₹20 lakh is ₹871.61 plus GST. Separately, GeM bid documents from one central institute (SCTIMST, 2022–25, snippets only) say sellers are registered "based on self declaration of valid Medical Device License" and the buyer must verify it.
- Source: GeM/2024/Vendor Validation Policy V 3.2 – https://assets-bg.gem.gov.in/resources/upload/shared_doc/gem-2024-vendor-validation-policy-v-3_1704955566.pdf – grade A (read in full, 5 pages). SCTIMST GeM bids – snippets only, grade A source.
- Status: CONFIRMED (primary source) for the policy wording. **We did not find one sentence in GeM policy that says "no listing without a CDSCO licence"**; that is our reading of the two sources together.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: plan on "licence first, GeM second". Listing itself is cheap. Our reading; confirm with a regulatory consultant.

### B2-6: Central rules allow EMD, turnover and experience to be waived for DPIIT-recognised startups
- Detail: The Startup India portal lists: GFR 2017 Rule 170(i), relief from EMD for DPIIT-recognised startups; Rule 173(i), relaxation of prior experience and prior turnover. On GeM, recognised startups are stated to be exempt from prior experience, prior turnover and EMD, get a "Startup India" badge, and can use "GeM Startup Runway" for products that are unique in design or function. GFR applies to central ministries and their public companies only. States named with their own orders: Odisha (13 Mar 2018; no minimum turnover, EMD exempt, performance security cut to 25%), Gujarat (11 Apr 2018) and Haryana (3 Jan 2019). The underlying finance-ministry order is dated 20 Sep 2016 (snippet only).
- Source: "Procurement by Government", Startup India (DPIIT) – https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/public_procurement.html – grade A
- Status: CONFIRMED (primary source)
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: once registered and DPIIT-recognised, lack of a sales history does not automatically shut us out of central tenders. **Kerala and Tamil Nadu were not on the list we saw.**

### B2-7: A hospital can still refuse the startup relaxation: Delhi High Court, July 2022
- Detail: In *Triveni Healthcure Pvt Ltd v. GNCTD* (2022 LiveLaw (Del) 645, reported 13 Jul 2022), a startup challenged two tenders for cardiology consumables at the Delhi government's Rajiv Gandhi Super Speciality Hospital. The court dismissed the case. It held the 2016 circular is permissive: "The executive instruction does not make it mandatory for any establishment to relax the condition". It stressed that this matters "especially in healthcare". A 2025 Jammu & Kashmir High Court ruling on a state medical-supplies tender reached the same view (snippet only).
- Source: LiveLaw report – https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/delhi-high-court-start-ups-tender-relaxation-healthcare-turnover-past-experience-bid-security-203668 – grade B (news report of a judgment)
- Status: CONFIRMED (two courts; second is snippet only)
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: never tell a judge "startups are exempt from tender conditions". Say "buyers may relax them".

### B2-8: Preference for MSEs goes to makers, not traders
- Detail: GeM bid documents state that traders are excluded from the Public Procurement Policy for Micro and Small Enterprises; to claim the preference the bidder must be the maker (OEM) of the product. Bidders claiming a turnover or experience exemption must upload proof.
- Source: SCTIMST GeM bid documents 2022–25 – **snippets only** – grade A source
- Status: SINGLE SOURCE (snippet)
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: we should be the legal manufacturer (even if assembly is contracted out), not a reseller of someone else's device.

### B2-9: State medical corporations (KMSCL, TNMSC): tender terms not found
- Detail: KMSCL is a Government of Kerala company (set up 2007) that buys for state health institutions. Aggregator listings of 2026 KMSCL equipment tenders show an EMD (for example ₹11,000 on a syringe-pump tender, ₹5,00,000 on an MRI tender) with "EMD exemption: allowed", but do not say who qualifies. - Source: tenders.infralens.in listings – **snippets only** – grade C

### B2-10: HLL Lifecare and its arm HITES act as buying agents for the Health Ministry
- Detail: HLL's Procurement and Consultancy division is described as a "National Procurement Support Agency". HITES (HLL Infra Tech Services) signed a 2017 agreement with AIIMS to procure equipment for the National Cancer Institute, Jhajjar, reported at ₹729 crore. Current (2026) status not checked.

### B2-10a: Anyone who stocks or sells a medical device needs a registration (Form MD-42)
- Detail: Central notification GSR 754(E) of 30 Sep 2022 requires anyone who sells, stocks, exhibits or distributes medical devices to hold a registration certificate in Form MD-42 from the state licensing authority, unless they already hold a licence under Rule 87(1) of the Medical Devices Rules 2017. Goa's drug regulator gave suppliers 15 days to comply (notice of 9 Nov 2022).
- Source: Goa Department of Information and Publicity press release, 9 Nov 2022 – https://dip.goa.gov.in/mandatory-registration-certificate-to-sell-stock-exhibit-offer-for-sale-distribute-medical-devices/ – grade A

- What it means for SpiroBuddy: a distributor or pharmacy that carries our device needs MD-42. Ask every channel partner for theirs.

### 4. Pricing and business-model options

### B2-17: Trade margins on medical devices in India are wide and mostly uncapped
- Detail: In 2021 the price regulator NPPA capped the trade margin (the gap between the price to the distributor and the printed maximum price) at 70% for six devices: oxygen concentrators (order of 3 Jun 2021) and pulse oximeters, blood-pressure monitors, nebulisers, digital thermometers and glucometers (13 Jul 2021). Before the cap, NPPA found margins from **3% to 709%**. A 2017 Maharashtra FDA study of 12 hospitals found distributor margins of 20%–211% on one heart-procedure device (snippet only). **We found no source for a "typical" dealer margin on a low-cost hospital device.**
- Source: "NPPA caps trade margin for six essential medical devices", LiveLaw (law-firm article), 2021 – https://www.livelaw.in/law-firms/law-firm-articles-/the-national-pharmaceuticals-pricing-authority-essential-medical-devices-trade-margin-178031 – grade B
- Status: CONFIRMED for the 70% cap and the 3%–709% range (also in legal500 and pharmatutor snippets). "Typical dealer margin": NOT CONFIRMED.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: if we ever sell through dealers, the margin is a negotiation, not a standard. Ask two distributors for their real terms before pricing.

In 2026 Haryana asked companies to equip 23 district hospitals, with CSR funds going to the state medical corporation (snippet only).

### B2-20: Why "lent, reused, paid per episode" fits
- Detail (team reasoning, using the report's estimates): parts cost ₹1,000–1,700 per unit. At the report's price idea of ₹500–1,000 per episode, one unit's parts are covered after about 2–4 paid episodes (₹1,700 ÷ ₹500 ≈ 3.4; ₹1,000 ÷ ₹500 = 2). Running costs per episode in the report are small: about ₹100–170 hardware share, ₹5 WhatsApp, ₹8 if an AI call is used. That leaves roughly ₹300–900 per episode **before** staff, cleaning, lost units, servers and regulatory cost, none of which is estimated yet. A one-time sale would earn the margin once and stop.
- Source: RESEARCH_REPORT.md sections 8.2 and 8.3 (team estimates, not validated with any hospital)
- Status: NOT CONFIRMED (arithmetic on our own estimates)
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: say "we expect a unit to pay back its parts within a handful of patients", and say it is an estimate.

### 6. First 10 customers

### B2-24: A step-by-step sequence for a student team with no company (team plan, built on the findings above)

| Step | When | What we do | Who we approach | What we ask for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Listen | Weeks 1–4 | 10 short interviews using the existing interview guide. No selling. | Head of cardiothoracic surgery or cardiac anaesthesia; head physiotherapist; nursing superintendent of the cardiac or surgical ward; biomedical engineering head, in **private, trust or medical-college teaching hospitals** near the team | One named **clinical champion** willing to be principal investigator |
| 2. Paper | Weeks 4–8 | With the champion: a one-page pilot agreement and a study plan for the hospital's ethics committee | The champion and the hospital's research office | A signed **letter of intent**, and a slot at the ethics committee |
| 3. Build | Weeks 4–12 | 5–10 working hardware units; bench-test accuracy | In-house; college lab or incubator | Incubator seat; small grant |
| 4. Free pilot | Months 3–6 | About 50 patients (report 8.5), 8–12 weeks, the staged plan in B2-16 | The champion's ward only | Weekly 15-minute review; permission to publish anonymised results |
| 5. Prove and convert | Months 6–9 | A 2-page results sheet: share of prescribed sessions done, nurse minutes saved, device loss rate, patient and nurse feedback. Offer per-episode pricing **to start when the licence is granted** | Champion + medical superintendent + purchase head | A paid-conversion letter conditional on licence; two introductions to peer hospitals |
| 6. Repeat | Months 6–18 | Sites 2–10 through the champion's introductions, IACTSCON (Feb 2027), one home-care company and one CSR donor for a government hospital | Peer department heads; CSR heads; Romsons | Pilot letters from 10 sites |

**What we offer in the free pilot:** units on loan; set-up and training in under an hour; a ward dashboard; a weekly summary for the champion; a co-authored poster or paper; no charge to hospital or patient.
**What the hospital gets as data:** anonymised adherence per patient and per ward, technique quality, which patients fell behind, nurse time spent. The hospital owns its patient data.
**What we ask in return:** a letter of intent on hospital letterhead; a named champion as principal investigator; ethics committee support; 30 minutes of nurse training time; honest feedback; the right to say "piloted at …" if results are positive.

**One-page pilot agreement: contents**
1. Parties, the ward, start and end dates (8–12 weeks), number of patients.
2. Purpose: a research pilot of a prototype. Not a sale. Not a diagnosis tool.
3. What each side provides (units, training, dashboard / a champion, a ward, nurse time).
4. Success measures agreed **before** the start, each with a number.
5. Money: free during the pilot; the price that will apply afterwards, conditional on the licence.
6. Data: patient consent in the patient's language; what is collected; who owns it; where it is stored; deletion at the end; DPDP compliance (report 7.2).
7. Safety: SpiroBuddy never replaces nurse checks; how problems are reported; who can stop the pilot.
8. Ethics committee approval number, and any regulator permission needed.
9. Device loss or damage: at our cost during the pilot.
10. Publication and use of the hospital's name. Signatures.

- Source: team plan; draws on B2-3, B2-11 to B2-16, report 8.5
- Status: NOT CONFIRMED (a plan, not a finding). Not legal advice: have a lawyer read the agreement.
- What it means for SpiroBuddy: the scarce thing is a champion, not a customer. Step 1 can begin the week after the hackathon.

## What to say in the 5-minute pitch
- "We start with private and teaching hospitals, where one department head can say yes. Over 90% of government medtech buying is by price tender, which does not suit a new kind of product." (B2-1, BioSpectrum 2019, industry opinion)
- "The hospital does not buy anything. We lend the unit and charge per patient, the way Tricog charges per ECG and Qure.ai per scan." (B2-13, B2-14)
- "For government hospitals we go the Dozee MillionICU way: a CSR donor pays. Indian companies spent about ₹34,900 crore on CSR in 2023–24." (B2-12, B2-18)
- "Once we are a registered startup with a device licence, central rules let government buyers waive deposit, turnover and experience conditions for us." (B2-5, B2-6: Startup India portal; GFR 2017 rules 170(i), 173(i))
- "Our next 90 days: ten hospital interviews, one clinical champion, one signed pilot letter." (B2-24, team plan)

## What NOT to say
- **"We are selling" or "we have customers / pilots / letters of intent."** We have none.
- **"Startups are exempt from tender conditions."** Buyers *may* relax them; the Delhi High Court said a hospital can refuse (B2-7).
- **"We will list on GeM next month."** No company, no DPIIT recognition, no device licence yet (B2-5).
- **Any price for Dozee, Cloudphysician or Stasis**, or "hospitals pay ₹X per bed". None is public.
- **"Dealer margins are 20–30%."** We found no source for a typical figure; published margins range from 3% to 709% (B2-17).
- **"Hospitals take 6 months to decide" or "half of pilots convert"** as facts. Both come from unsourced blogs (B2-3).
- **"Outcome-based pricing"** or "we get paid when complications fall". No Indian example, and we cannot prove outcomes (report 2.4).
- **"Romsons is our partner"** or any association endorsement. No contact has been made.
- **"Dozee saves 2.5 hours of nursing time per patient"** as an independent fact. It is a company claim, and an earlier company figure was 30 minutes.
