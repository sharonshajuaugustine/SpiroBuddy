# SpiroBuddy – business brief (one page from five research files)

**Written:** 10 Oct 2026. Built from the five files in this folder (89 findings, about 90 newly opened sources, all listed in `docs/research/SOURCES_LEDGER.md` from #232). Each line names its finding, for example `B2-11`. **This is a map with sources, not legal, medical or investment advice.** Prices and reuse counts are our own estimates until a hospital says otherwise.

## The story in six lines
1. **Customer:** the hospital buys; the head of surgery or physiotherapy asks for it; the purchase head signs small items. (01)
2. **Start:** heart-surgery units in mid-size private or mission hospitals, piloted through one teaching hospital, in the state the team lives in. Kerala has startup-friendly state buying rules (not confirmed current). (01)
3. **We cannot sell yet.** It is a measuring device, so it needs a manufacturing licence. Until then every deployment is a free, ethics-approved research pilot. (02, 04)
4. **Business model:** lend the unit, charge the hospital per patient episode (about ₹500); donor or CSR money for government hospitals. (02)
5. **Money first comes from grants, not investors:** NIDHI-PRAYAS (up to ₹10 lakh, individuals), then BIRAC BIG (up to ₹50 lakh). (03)
6. **Evidence in order:** bench test → healthy volunteers → 30–50 patient pilot → controlled study. (05)

## Market size (per year, India) – every figure rests on one big guess
| | Cautious | Middle | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| All India | ₹24.5 crore | ₹73.5 crore | ₹147 crore |
| Private hospitals, 5 southern states | ₹6.1 crore | ₹18.2 crore | ₹36.4 crore |
| What we could win by year 3 | ₹3 lakh | ₹37.5 lakh | ₹2 crore |

The guess: 10%, 20% or 30% of India's 49 lakh major operations a year come with a spirometer. Nobody publishes this. **Post-surgery alone is a small market**; the growth story is lung rehabilitation after TB and for long-term lung disease. (01)

## Unit economics (14-day episode, ₹1,500 unit reused 10 times – team estimates)
| Price per episode | At 50 patients (pilot) | At 5,000 patients |
|---|---|---|
| ₹300 | loss | about −₹18 |
| ₹500 | loss (server bill dominates) | about +₹182 |
| ₹1,000 | profit | about +₹682 |

Cost per episode is about ₹505 at pilot size and about ₹318 at scale. At ₹500 a unit pays for itself after about 5 patients. (03)

## The licence path and its official fees (04)
| Step | Fee | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Test licence for prototypes | ₹500 per device | 30 days |
| Clinical investigation permission, if needed | ₹1,00,000 (nil in government hospitals) | 90 days |
| Manufacturing licence, state authority | ₹5,000 + ₹500 | about 45 days to 5 months |

The real cost is testing, a quality system and a consultant. No prices for those were found.

## The one question that blocks everything
**Does a first patient pilot with an unlicensed prototype need CDSCO permission, or only an ethics committee?** Three of the five agents hit this and none could confirm it. The "ethics committee only" rule covers academic studies on *licensed* devices. Ask a regulatory consultant first. Safest route meanwhile: a **hospital-led** study with a doctor as lead investigator. (02, 04, 05)

## ICMR, plainly (05)
- **No connection today, and ICMR does not approve apps or devices.** CDSCO licenses devices.
- Real touchpoints: the 2017 ethics guidelines, the 2023 AI-in-healthcare guidelines, the trial register (CTRI: free, about 10 working days), and innovator support (MedTech Mitra, Patent Mitra, grants).
- Our biggest gap against the AI guidelines: **the model has only seen practice breaths, never real ones.**
- The national health ID system (ABDM) is not needed for a pilot.

## What is sobering (say it before a judge does)
- Dozee, the best-known Indian company here, raised about US$20 million and earned ₹5.2 crore against a ₹68 crore loss. Hospitals pay little for monitoring. (01)
- First paid use took about 14 months (Qure.ai) to about 4 years (Dozee). (02)
- Government buyers mostly choose the lowest price and can pay up to about 2 years late. (02)
- No Indian medical-society guideline recommending the spirometer was found. (05)

## Never say
"Prevents pneumonia" (in advertising this is a criminal offence under the 1954 health-claims law, B4), "ICMR approved", "ABDM compliant", "clinically validated", "first", "we are eligible for the Startup India Seed Fund".

## First 90 days
1. **10 listening interviews**; find one doctor or senior nurse as champion. (Also today's hackathon requirement.)
2. **Regulatory consultant:** confirm the device class and the pilot-permission question.
3. **Apply to NIDHI-PRAYAS** (students need a no-objection letter from the college); KSUM Idea Grant if in Kerala. Mind the order: more than ₹10 lakh of other government money can block the Startup India Seed Fund.
4. **Build 5–10 units**; bench-test the sensor against the printed marks.
5. **Ethics committee submission** at one teaching hospital: protocol, information sheet, consent form with a literate witness, insurance.
6. **One-page pilot agreement and a letter of intent.** Check the college's and the hackathon's rules on who owns student work.
