# Informal nurse feedback and submission wording

Recorded: 12 October 2026.

## Provenance

The team member reports that his mother is a nurse and relayed the following observations. On 12 October 2026, he additionally reported calling her and receiving confirmation of the three concerns below that had emerged from public discussions. These are second-hand informal feedback, not a recorded interview, a structured study, or verified clinical evidence. No hospital, name or years of experience has been supplied. Do not invent these details or attribute individual Reddit accounts to her.

## Reported observations

- Older patients and other patients sometimes skip prescribed breathing exercises when they hurt.
- She observes that recovery can take longer in these situations. This is an anecdotal observation; it does not establish causation or demonstrate that SpiroBuddy improves recovery.
- She calls patients to remind or clarify forgotten medicine prescriptions and instructions.
- The team member reports that she also agrees patients can find discharge paperwork confusing.
- He reports that she agrees patients can be uncertain about correct breathing-exercise technique.
- He reports that she agrees staff have limited time for repeated patient coaching.

## Product implications (team interpretation)

- Check-ins should ask why a report/session is missing rather than assume forgetfulness or non-adherence.
- Pain-related concerns should be routed to the care team; automation should not pressure patients to continue or modify prescribed treatment.
- Patients need a readable, persistent copy of their clinician-approved medicine instructions.
- Staff need a clear follow-up queue. Reduction in workload must be measured rather than claimed.

## Claims to avoid

- “We interviewed nurses” or “a hospital study” without supporting evidence.
- Attributing individual Reddit stories, medical explanations or quotations to the mother. Confirmation applies only to the concerns the team member reported discussing.
- Claiming measured faster recovery, fewer complications, fewer readmissions or reduced nursing workload.
- Claiming a missing report proves that a prescribed exercise or medicine was skipped.
